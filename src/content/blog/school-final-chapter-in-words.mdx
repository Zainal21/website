Assalamualaikum Warahmatullahi Wabarakatuh, hello everyone! 👋

I hope you're all doing well. The year 2021 marked the end of my school journey. It also happened to coincide with the turning point where real-life decisions start to matter — and, if I'm being honest, the pandemic quietly stole some of the memories school should have left me with. 😢

Let me tell you about the last three years before the "what now?" begins.

## Where it all started: tenth grade

Coming into high school with a basic background in IT, I adapted quickly to the field of Software Engineering (RPL — Rekayasa Perangkat Lunak). The first year spread across three main areas:

- **Graphic Design** — where I learned to think about composition and visual hierarchy.
- **Basic Computer and Networking** — the plumbing that makes everything talk to each other.
- **Basic Programming** — the part that turned out to be my favorite.

Programming gave me the feeling of building something from nothing. In tenth grade I worked with **VB.Net on Visual Studio**, building small but satisfying applications: a calculator, a number-guessing game, and a simple cashier app. Nothing impressive by today's standards, but those little apps were where the bug bit me.

## The middle year everyone warns you about

The second year is the one people joke about as the hardest — the "tricky sophomore" year. For me, it was the most exciting one. I genuinely enjoyed the puzzles that programming kept throwing at me. Every bug was a riddle, every feature a small victory.

Were there complaints? Yes — mostly about the sheer volume of assignments. There were nights where I forgot to sleep because I was chasing an error down to its roots, or trying to make one more feature work. A good kind of problem, I suppose.

## Twelfth grade: where it got serious

In my final year I went deeper into the subjects that matter most in this industry:

- **Object-Oriented Programming (OOP)** — writing code that models real things, not just instructions.
- **Web Programming** — the craft of building for the browser.
- **System Analysis** — understanding problems *before* writing a line of code.

I made a choice here: I leaned hard into **web development**. I had already played with HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and PHP, and eventually explored **CodeIgniter 3** and **Laravel**.

Then came February, and my internship. I joined a team as a **junior web developer**, building an **online wedding invitation** and a **Village Information System** — both with Laravel. The experience was exactly what I needed: real projects, real deadlines, real stakes.

Unfortunately, the COVID-19 outbreak pulled the rug from under it. The internship moved online, then quietly faded. The rest of twelfth grade was a blur of remote learning, and honestly, that's the part of school that left the least impression — not because the material was weak, but because being present matters more than we'd like to admit.

I also competed at the **provincial level** of the Student Competency Competition (LKS) in Web Technologies, representing my district and school. I didn't win, but I never regretted it. The stage, the pressure, the other competitors — all of it was a lesson classroom work can't give you.

## The "what now?" dilemma

'After school' comes with an uncomfortable choice. College? Work? Or, as some of my friends teased, marriage?

I chose **work first**. Two reasons:

1. I wanted to contribute to my family's happiness as soon as I could.
2. I wanted to keep learning — but in the real world, on real projects, rather than in another classroom.

Why not college? The honest answer is that the skills from high school felt sufficient for where I wanted to start, and I've always learned fastest by doing. That might change, and I'm not closing the door. But for now, building beats studying-about-building.

Marriage? Not anytime soon — maybe seven or eight years down the road. I'm not ready yet, and frankly there's no candidate lined up either. Pray I find the right and righteous one when the time comes.

## The point

Your decisions deserve thought — not fear, but thought. Stay motivated, aim at the dreams you actually want, and accept this early: **the road will not be as smooth as you imagine.** That's not pessimism. That's a warning so you stop being surprised when bumps appear.

Stay positive. Be grateful. Keep moving.

## Hope for us all

To everyone graduating in 2021: don't celebrate too soon. Real life is about to begin — and it's heavier and more interesting than you expect. Prepare yourselves, stay motivated, don't give up easily, be grateful, pray, and keep your head down in worship.

May you all succeed, and may you be useful — to your religion, your country, and your family. Aamiin. 🙌
